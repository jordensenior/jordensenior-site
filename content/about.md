I make images where light, time and chemistry are allowed to misbehave—on purpose. I’m very interested in the materiality of photographic image making, both in analogue, digital, and hybrid workflows. I see process as a medium in itself.

On the analogue side I print everything by hand, pushing materials to their limits, e.g. through: mordançage (B/W and RA4), lith, chromoskedasic and classic Sabattier, lumen exposures and other darkroom deviations. On the digital side I explore intentional camera movement (ICM), slit‑scan and other time‑based captures. Outputs range from fibre and RC papers to thermal‑paper prints, two‑colour Risograph and small‑run zines. I tune paper, chemistry and software for how they behave under stress; imperfections are evidence, not errors. 

My day job is building superconducting quantum hardware. I don’t illustrate physics; I borrow its mindset—careful instrumentation, sensitivity to initial conditions and the knowledge that observation changes the system. I’ve also spent years in the cleanroom, which—like the darkroom—demands environmental control, repeatability and patience; it’s likely why these methods clicked. I like to think my work goes towards *entangling quantum and art*.

Alongside the prints I write about the philosophy of photography, and I sometimes pair series with music that carries the same tension and weight. I also prototype small quantum games and interactive sketches—systems that play with measurement, noise and time—often made for jams.

This site is my working notebook: finished series alongside experiments, game prototypes, process notes, failures and the occasional musing on images, materials and sound. It will change as the work does.

Based in Helsinki, Finland.
Made in the North of England.
